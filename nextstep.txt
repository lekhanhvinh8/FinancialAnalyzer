=== DONE: P/E sell gate (SELL_MIN_PELOG) — swept, refuted ===

Blocks a sell while ln(P/E / its own 500-bar median) is below a threshold, i.e.
while the P/E is not stretched. 52 symbols, 16 thresholds vs off. 0/16 beat off,
13/16 have a paired median of exactly 0.00.

The mechanism works and is not the problem. At 0.30: FPT -85.0 -> +3.2, LPB +116,
DPM +108, GMD +96, CTG +93. LPB had never moved for anything before. But 19 up
against 21 down, and the losses are bigger and more concentrated: HPG -340 (36%
of all damage), VND -197, SHB -138. +833 against -945, net -112.

Not selective, just quiet: FPT's fix is its sell count going 4 -> 0, and VND
loses 197 with its sell count unchanged at 1, meaning the gate delayed the sell
onto a worse day rather than preventing it.

THIRD attempt at the same thing, all three refuted the same way:
  SELL_MIN_ZDEV                     0 of 66 cells
  PERCENT_SELL x PERCENT_REINVEST   FPT +91, GMD +428  -> 15 up, 34 down
  SELL_MIN_PELOG                    FPT +88, GMD  +96  -> 19 up, 21 down

Recorded in sell.py for whoever considers a fourth. The sell arm is net positive
across the portfolio; restraining it pays the majority to rescue the minority.

Still open:
  - The FPT problem is real and unfixed: 85 points, and the 400-bar reinvest
    timeout buying back higher than it sold. What the evidence asks for is
    something that separates a compounder from an oscillator PER SYMBOL. No
    portfolio-wide threshold on one signal can do that.
  - The z-score form of pelog is measured and untried (uniform across symbols:
    every symbol fires 1.5%-8.4% at a common threshold, against 17 near-zero for
    the level form). Cheap to try, but it answers "unusual lately" rather than
    "expensive", and z-scores have not worked in this repo.

=== DONE: 2026-08-18 price repair — every number before this date was measured on a broken series ===

Nine symbols were stored on a pre-adjustment price scale. The source back-adjusts
its whole history on a bonus issue or stock dividend; sync_prices only ever
appended new dates and protected the rows it already had, so the series kept the
old scale and broke by a constant factor at the ex-date.

    VHM 2.001x  SSI 1.251x  MBB 1.202x  SAB 1.069x  BID 1.068x
    DPM 1.068x  PLX 1.036x  MWG 1.016x  BSR 1.011x (unnoticed since 2020)

VHM's break was a fake -49.9% crash on 2026-07-31, which is what surfaced it.

Fixed in e12842d (detect it at sync time), 4bf26c9 (the same problem one layer
down, when a late balance sheet restates the share count), b8d31b6 (an index
aggregate built from 8 of 30 members, and an audit that only looked at a window).
scripts/audit_data.py checks prices and shares against the source and pe_ratios
against price x shares, and repairs what it finds with --fix. Both audits ran
clean afterwards, source-side included.

WHAT IT CHANGED

    run_all, 52 symbols     before      after
    median diff             +20.14%     +16.16%
    mean diff               +52.38%     +47.18%
    net                     +2724       +2454
    improved                40/52       38/52

The edge shrank by about a fifth and survived. VHM is most of it: +132.37 ->
-36.28, because its buy-and-hold doubled once the fake crash was gone. Its
strategy return did not move (196.95 -> 197.00) — the arm reads distance from a
rolling median, which a rescale leaves alone.

PERCENT_SELL was re-swept and did not move at all: the 65-85 region holds cell
for cell, no p changed in three decimals. The reasoning, and why it does not
carry to every result, is in sweep_percent_sell.py's docstring.

Still open:
  - The other sweeps were not re-run. Same argument should hold for any of them
    quoting the vs-live column, since a per-symbol constant cancels out of a
    paired difference. It does NOT hold for anything quoted against buy-and-hold,
    and no sweep here was checked for that.
  - LPB +17.56, STB -17.28 and ACB +7.67 moved in run_all without being in the
    repaired nine. Two new bars (08-17, 08-18) flipping a late trade would
    explain it, but 17 points off two bars is a lot to assume.
