=== DONE: portfolio/ — a second investment method, one wallet over the basket ===

single/ asks "does this rule beat buy-and-hold on FPT", once per symbol, with a
separate wallet each time. portfolio/ asks "given one monthly contribution and the
whole basket to choose from, where should it go". That is not a loop over the
first question, because the money is shared and the symbols compete for it.

Buy only. The sell and reinvest arms exist, run every day, and decline — their
rule is NeverRule, a real rule rather than a special case, so switching one on is
a config change.

THE DECISION THAT HAS NO PER-SYMBOL EQUIVALENT

  A per-symbol strategy fuses three things because with one candidate they are
  one thing: eligibility is the whole decision and the size is "whatever cash
  there is". With a shared wallet they come apart:

      rules.py       who is eligible today
      scoring.py     in what order they deserve the money
      allocator.py   how much each one gets

  The middle one is new. It did not exist before and it is where the interesting
  design questions live — rank by discount to median, by drawdown, by momentum?

INDEPENDENT OF single/, ON PURPOSE

  Nothing in portfolio/ imports anything from single/. Two earlier drafts did —
  first by adding score() methods to the strategy classes, then by calling
  make_median_strategies() — and both were wrong for the same reason: they made
  an experiment in the new method able to disturb results in the old one, which
  carries years of measured findings.

  What IS shared is the database, and only the database. Prices, P/E and rolling
  statistics are facts about the market, not part of any evaluation method; a
  second definition of them is how two halves of a project start disagreeing
  about reality.

FIRST RESULTS

    4 symbols (FPT HPG STB VNM)     49 symbols
    plan      217.38%               144.87%
    DCA       188.60%               132.68%
    diff      +28.77%               +12.20%

  The gap between the two columns is the finding, not either number. With
  TOP_K=None the contribution is split equally over every candidate, and the buy
  condition admits most of the universe on an ordinary day — so the ranking is
  inert and the method approximates buying everything. TOP_K is the first thing
  to sweep, and SCORER only means anything once TOP_K is set.

TWO BUGS FOUND WHILE BUILDING IT

  1. GHOST TRADES. The first run reported 2,973 buys on four symbols over 136
     months; 462 of FPT's 542 were 2.26e-313 VND. 8,000,000 / 3 is not exact in
     binary, the residue left the wallet marginally above zero, and the next day
     the allocator split the residue and the engine booked another trade. No
     return was affected — the final value did not move by one dong when it was
     fixed, which is what confirmed the diagnosis — but the trade counts were
     wrong and a cost model would have charged brokerage on every ghost.
     engine.CASH_EPSILON = 1 VND. The baseline never showed it: 8,000,000 / 4 is
     exact.

  2. THE WARMUP BOUNDARY. range_stats is built with min_periods=1, so row 0 holds
     the median of one observation and only row `window - 1` has a full window
     behind it. single/ requires index > window, which is two bars late; the
     first draft here used index >= window, one bar late. Neither is right and
     the error is silent — it moves the first purchase to a different day at a
     different price and every number downstream shifts with no indication why.
     portfolio/ now starts at window - 1.

  Also worth recording: prices are REAL VND here. range_stats stores price * 1000
  and single/ multiplies its closes to match; the factor cancels out of every
  return, so nothing there was ever wrong, but it makes reported share counts a
  thousandth of the truth. Verified over FPT's 3,818 rows that the two scalings
  agree on every single buy decision, max difference 1.4e-14.

  3. THE COST BASIS WAS THE WRONG ONE. Position carried `contributed` — "every
     dong ever put into this symbol, never reduced by a sale" — and summarise
     added it to reserve_basis to get the capital invested. After a sale the same
     money sits in both, so the total was overstated by whatever was waiting in
     the reserve. Buy 10m, sell for 20m: invested reads 20m and the return reads
     0%.

     single/models.py already had the answer and it was not copied: totalCapital
     is the original capital and DECREASES on a sale, while totalCurrentCapital
     is what the current holding cost. Position now carries the same pair as
     basis / basis_current. basis follows the money — out on a sale, into
     reserve_basis, back proportionally on a reinvest — so summing it cannot
     double-count, and it is what the reported return is measured against.

     Sell 18m of original capital for 40m and put all 40m into another name:
     basis 18m, basis_current 40m. The recycled 22m of profit is not capital the
     investor put in, and counting it as such would report the same gain twice.

     No published number changes: with the sell arm off, basis and basis_current
     are equal everywhere and the reserve is always empty. Verified — the
     four-symbol run is 217.38% / 188.60% before and after.

Still open:
  - TOP_K x SCORER sweep. One axis, not two: the scorer is inert without a cutoff.
  - Loading dominates — 296s for 49 symbols against 0.4s for the backtest. A sweep
    must load once and iterate cells over one Market, which also means
    parallelising over CELLS rather than symbols, the opposite of run_all.py.
  - No chart. export/chart.py is single-symbol; a portfolio wants an equity curve,
    allocation over time and per-symbol contribution.
  - Costs are zero — no brokerage, no 0.1% sell tax, no lot size, no settlement.
    Same as single/ so the two stay comparable, but a portfolio trades far more
    often, so every return here is an upper bound.
  - Survivorship. UNIVERSE="all" is the symbols worth collecting TODAY. Naming an
    index is not a fix. Point-in-time membership is, and the data is not there:
    index_members has 5 join dates for vn30 and no departures for vnfinlead.
  - When a rule here starts reading a signal with its own start date, the warmup
    rule in data.py has to grow up. It is deliberately just max(window) - 1 today
    because there are no signals.

=== DONE: benchmark is VN30 itself, and the buy arm falls back to it ===

    UNIVERSE              = "vn30"      current members, 28 after EXCLUDE
    BENCHMARK             = "VN30"      the index, loaded as a tradable instrument
    FALLBACK_TO_BENCHMARK = True        no candidate -> buy the index

WHY THE BENCHMARK CHANGED, AND WHY IT MATTERS THIS MUCH

  Equal-weight DCA across the members is itself a strategy — a deliberate tilt
  away from capitalisation weighting that pays on its own. Measuring against it
  asked the plan to beat selection PLUS a weighting scheme, and no result could
  be attributed to either.

  The size of the effect is not subtle. Same plan, same money:

      vs equal-weight DCA over all 49 symbols     +12.20%
      vs DCA into VN30                            +94.59%

  Most of that gap is the benchmark, not the plan. VN30 the index returns 94.74%
  over the period while an equal-weighted basket of its members returns far more,
  which is the small-cap tilt showing up as a number. Nothing from the old
  baseline is comparable with anything from the new one.

WHY THE FALLBACK

  A strict rule with no fallback is two bets at once: which stocks, and whether
  to be in the market at all. When nobody qualifies the cash waits, and the
  comparison quietly becomes stocks-versus-cash. Buying the index instead keeps
  the plan invested on exactly the bars the benchmark is, so the difference is
  attributable to the choosing.

  Verified across thresholds — the fallback is dormant where the rule is
  permissive and takes over where it is not:

      BUY_MAX_PREMIUM   fallback buys   total buys   growth
             60               0            1,946     189.33%
             20               3            1,170     190.09%
              0              11              588     187.90%
            -20              51              215     155.51%
            -40             112              129     144.55%
      DCA VN30              n/a              127      94.74%

  At -40 the plan is 112/129 index purchases and still returns 144.55%, because
  the 17 picks it did make were bought at deep discounts.

MECHANICS

  rules.SymbolIn is the one rule that keys on WHICH symbol rather than on price.
  It does two jobs: the baseline is SymbolIn({"VN30"}) alone, and the plan wraps
  its condition in AllOf([Not(SymbolIn({"VN30"})), ...]) so the index is never an
  ordinary candidate. Without that the plan would hold the index alongside its
  own constituents and be partly holding the thing it is measured against.

  universe.resolve() gained `extra=`, appended AFTER the exclusion filter, so the
  benchmark is always loaded even though it is not a candidate.

Still open:
  - n = 1 remains the real problem. One run is one number with no error bar, so
    a 3-point move from a parameter change cannot be told from noise. Rolling
    start dates (one run per quarter, ~40 paired observations) is the fix and is
    cheap: the Market loads once, each run is 0.4s.
  - No equity curve, no max drawdown, no per-symbol attribution.
